Frame-Consistent Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting from Sparse Unposed Images

Viruthshaan Vinasirajan, Ranga Rodrigo

A frozen VGGT pose estimator and a frozen TokenGS reconstructor, connected with three deterministic pose fixes. No training, no per-scene optimisation, no COLMAP at test time.

Abstract

Feed-forward Gaussian models have made novel view synthesis almost instantaneous. Using two separately trained models, one for pose estimation and one for reconstruction, is attractive because each can specialise in its own objective. A natural instantiation pairs a frozen pose estimator, VGGT, with a frozen reconstruction backbone, TokenGS. Prior two-stage work attributes the resulting quality degradation to noisy pose estimates and introduces learned refinement. We show that this is not the primary source of the problem.

Through per-camera analysis across 107 scenes from DL3DV and MipNeRF360, using COLMAP poses as the reference, we find that the rotation error after alignment is small, whereas the translation scale is offset by a large, dataset-consistent factor. Controlled ablations on the DL3DV benchmark show that the obstacles are integration failures rather than pose error: a coordinate-frame inconsistency between context and target views, a camera-convention mismatch, and a translation-scale mismatch, all of which can be corrected deterministically. With these fixes a frozen VGGT driving a frozen TokenGS reaches 22.47 PSNR, within 0.5 dB of the COLMAP reference, with no additional training.

Method

Pipeline figure (Fig. 2 of the paper)

The whole method is pose post-processing between the two frozen models:

  1. Frame-consistent inference. Run VGGT once over context and target views together, so every camera lands in one shared frame. Running it on the context views alone and borrowing target poses from elsewhere puts the two sets in unrelated frames.
  2. Convention conversion. VGGT emits OpenCV camera axes; TokenGS was trained on NeRF-style transforms. Flip the y and z columns of every camera-to-world matrix.
  3. Translation scale. VGGT's output is scale-normalised. Multiply translations by a fixed 7.0, the median COLMAP-to-VGGT baseline ratio over 100 DL3DV training scenes, or normalise the first-camera baseline to 1 for a calibration-free variant.
